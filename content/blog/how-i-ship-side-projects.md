# How I Ship Side Projects That Actually Get Used

We have all been there: a spark of inspiration, a weekend of coding, and a repository that eventually gathers dust in your GitHub profile. Shipping projects that people actually use requires shifting your focus from "coding a tool" to "solving a problem."

Here is my personal playbook for shipping successful side projects:

## 1. Solve Your Own Friction First

The best ideas come from personal problems. If you face a friction point daily, chances are others do too. Building for yourself guarantees at least one active user (you) and provides a clear feedback loop.

## 2. Define a Strict Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

It is easy to get distracted by features. List all your ideas, then cut 80% of them. Your MVP should do exactly **one** thing exceptionally well.
- **Example:** For a domain monitoring tool, the core is simply alerting when a domain is expiring. Fancy analytical charts can wait.

## 3. Choose a Familiar Tech Stack

A side project is rarely the right place to learn three new frameworks simultaneously unless the goal is purely learning. If you want to launch, use what you know. This keeps your momentum high and time-to-market low.

## 4. Build in Public

Share your progress, design mockups, and technical challenges on platforms like Twitter/X or read-dev communities. It builds an audience before you even launch, making launch day much more impactful.
